Insulin degludec: Tresiba

insulin degludec · Ultra-long-acting (basal) insulin · Novo Nordisk

✓Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · Written by Dr. George Grunberger · Medical review: pending — clinician review in progress · How we review

In short

Tresiba (insulin degludec) is an ultra-long-acting (basal) insulin made by Novo Nordisk, used for basal blood sugar control in diabetes (adults and children 1+). Schedule: once daily. It is prescription-only in the US and Canada.

FormPens (U-100, U-200), vials
How oftenOnce daily

What it is used for

  • Basal blood sugar control in diabetes (adults and children 1+)

Tresiba dosage

Per the official label. Your prescriber sets your dose — never change it on your own.

Tresiba label dosing schedule
1DoseIndividualized in units; flexible timing, at least 8 hours between doses

How to take it

Under the skin once daily.

Storage

Unopened: refrigerate. In use: up to 56 days.

Side effects

Common

  • Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)
  • Injection-site reactions
  • Weight gain
  • Lipodystrophy or skin thickening at repeated injection sites

Serious — get medical help

  • Severe hypoglycemia (confusion, seizures, loss of consciousness)
  • Low potassium (hypokalemia)
  • Fluid retention and heart failure when combined with thiazolidinediones
  • Serious allergic reactions

Who should not use it

  • During an episode of low blood sugar
  • Never share pens or needles, even if the needle is changed
  • Do not switch insulin type, brand or concentration without your prescriber
