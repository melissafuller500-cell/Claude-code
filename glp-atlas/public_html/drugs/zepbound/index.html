Zepbound

tirzepatide · Dual GIP / GLP-1 receptor agonist · Eli Lilly

✓Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · Written by Dr. George Grunberger · Medical review: pending — clinician review in progress · How we review

In short

Zepbound (tirzepatide) is a dual GIP / GLP-1 receptor agonist made by Eli Lilly, used for weight reduction and long-term maintenance in adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. Schedule: once weekly. It is prescription-only in the US and Canada.

FormPrefilled pen or single-dose vial, subcutaneous injection
How oftenOnce weekly
United StatesFDA-approved.
CanadaThe Zepbound name is US-specific; in Canada tirzepatide is marketed as Mounjaro. Check the Drug Product Database.
PrescriptionRequired
⚠ Boxed warning: risk of thyroid C-cell tumors. Do not use if you or a family member has had medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or if you have Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).

What it is used for

  • Weight reduction and long-term maintenance in adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition
  • Moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity

Zepbound dosage

Per the official label. Your prescriber sets your dose — never change it on your own.

How to take it

Inject under the skin of the stomach, thigh or upper arm once weekly.

Storage

Refrigerate. Pens/vials may be kept unrefrigerated up to 86°F (30°C) for up to 21 days.

Side effects

Common

  • Nausea
  • Diarrhea
  • Vomiting
  • Constipation
  • Abdominal pain
  • Indigestion / heartburn
  • Fatigue
  • Decreased appetite

Serious — get medical help

  • Pancreatitis (severe stomach pain that may spread to the back)
  • Gallbladder problems, including gallstones
  • Low blood sugar when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney problems, often from dehydration after vomiting or diarrhea
  • Serious allergic reactions
  • Diabetic retinopathy complications (people with type 2 diabetes)
  • Pulmonary aspiration during anesthesia or deep sedation — tell your surgical team you take it

Who should not use it

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or MEN 2
  • Prior serious allergic reaction to the drug or its ingredients
  • Pregnancy (stop before a planned pregnancy as advised by your prescriber)
  • Use together with another GLP-1 receptor agonist

Cost

Frequently asked questions

Zepbound or Wegovy — which causes more weight loss?

In head-to-head data, tirzepatide produced more average weight loss than semaglutide 2.4 mg. Tolerance, coverage and medical history decide which suits a person.

Why are Zepbound vials cheaper?

Lilly sells single-dose vials directly to self-pay patients with a prescription through LillyDirect.
