Retatrutide in 2026: Trial Results and What's Next

✓Last reviewed October 20, 2026 · Written by Dr. George Grunberger · Medical review: pending — clinician review in progress · How we review

Retatrutide, Lilly's GIP/GLP-1/glucagon triple agonist: TRIUMPH phase 3 results from July 2026, the planned 2027 FDA filing, and why online 'reta' is unsafe.

3 min read

Key takeaways

Retatrutide is an investigational weekly injection that acts on three hormone receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. Lilly reported positive TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 phase 3 results in July 2026 and plans to submit to the FDA in Q1 2027. It is not approved anywhere.

Full Retatrutide reference: uses, dosing, side effects →

Why a triple agonist

Adding glucagon-receptor activity to GIP and GLP-1 is designed to increase energy use as well as reduce appetite. In the phase 2 trial, the highest dose produced average weight loss of about 24% at 48 weeks.

What is being studied

The TRIUMPH phase 3 program covers obesity, knee osteoarthritis pain and obstructive sleep apnea, among other conditions.

Timeline

  • July 2026: positive TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 results announced.
  • Q1 2027: planned FDA submission.
  • Approval would follow review — typically months later.

Why online “reta” is dangerous

Products sold as retatrutide online are unapproved, untested and of unknown content and dose. In August 2026 the FDA issued warning letters to online sellers of products marketed as retatrutide. Do not inject anything bought this way.

Frequently asked questions

Is retatrutide approved?

No. It is investigational, with an FDA submission planned for Q1 2027.

How is it different from tirzepatide?

Tirzepatide acts on GIP and GLP-1; retatrutide adds glucagon receptors.

How can I get it legally?

Only through a registered clinical trial — search ClinicalTrials.gov.
