**A failing ignition coil usually shows up as an engine misfire: a rough idle, hesitation or stumbling under load, reduced power, and often a flashing or steady check engine light with a misfire code. Confirm it by reading the stored codes, then moving the suspect coil to another cylinder and checking whether the misfire follows it.**

Ignition coils are among the most common causes of misfires on modern engines, but they are not the only cause. This guide covers the symptoms, a reliable way to confirm the fault, and how to decide between replacing one coil or the whole set.

## What does an ignition coil do?

An ignition coil turns the vehicle’s low battery voltage into the high voltage needed to fire the spark plug. Most modern engines use a coil-on-plug design, with one coil sitting directly on top of each spark plug. When one coil weakens or fails, the cylinder it serves stops firing properly, and the engine runs unevenly.

## What are the signs of a failing ignition coil?

Symptoms vary with how badly the coil has failed and how many cylinders the engine has. The most common signs are:

- **Rough idle:** the engine shakes or vibrates at a stop.
- **Hesitation or stumbling:** the engine stutters when accelerating or climbing a hill.
- **Loss of power:** the vehicle feels weaker than usual.
- **Check engine light:** a steady light, or a flashing light during driving.
- **Misfire codes:** stored diagnostic trouble codes that point to a misfire.
- **Worse fuel economy:** the engine uses more fuel to make the same power.
- **Hard starting:** in some cases, the engine takes longer to start.

A flashing check engine light usually means a severe misfire. Driving with a severe misfire can damage the catalytic converter, so the vehicle should be checked as soon as possible.

## What else can cause the same symptoms?

A misfire is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Before replacing a coil, keep the other common causes in mind:

| Possible cause | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Ignition coil | Misfire moves to another cylinder when the coil is moved |
| Spark plug | Worn, fouled, or damaged plug; misfire stays with the plug |
| Fuel injector | Misfire stays in the cylinder after coil and plug swaps |
| Vacuum leak | Lean condition; often affects more than one cylinder |
| Wiring or connector | Damaged harness, loose connector, or corrosion at the coil |
| Mechanical fault | Low compression in one cylinder |

This is why confirming the fault matters. Replacing a coil that is not the problem costs time and money and leaves the misfire in place.

## How do you confirm a bad ignition coil?

Always follow the vehicle maker’s service information for the specific engine, and work with the engine off and cool when removing components. A common, reliable sequence is:

1. **Read the codes.** Connect a scan tool and record every stored and pending code. On OBD-II vehicles, generic misfire codes run from P0300 (random or multiple misfires) to P0301, P0302, and so on, where the last digit is the cylinder number.
2. **Note which cylinder is misfiring.** If the scan tool shows live misfire counts, watch them at idle and under light load.
3. **Inspect the coil and connector.** Look for cracks in the coil body, carbon tracking, oil or coolant contamination, and loose or corroded connectors.
4. **Do a swap test.** Move the suspect coil to a cylinder that is running normally, and move that cylinder’s coil to the misfiring position.
5. **Clear the codes and retest.** Run the engine under the same conditions and read the codes again.
6. **Interpret the result.** If the misfire moves to the new cylinder, the coil is the cause. If it stays in the original cylinder, look at the spark plug, injector, wiring, or compression next.

The swap test is simple and needs no special equipment beyond a scan tool, which is why it is a favorite first step in many shops.

## Should you replace one coil or the whole set?

Both approaches are common, and the right choice depends on the vehicle and the customer.

- **Replace one coil** when a single coil has clearly failed, the others test well, and the customer wants to keep the cost down.
- **Replace the full set** on a higher-mileage engine where the remaining coils are the same age as the failed one, or when the customer wants to avoid a repeat visit.

Many technicians also inspect or replace the spark plugs at the same time, because the coils are already off and worn plugs put extra load on the coils.

## How do you choose the right replacement coil?

Coils differ by engine, connector, and mounting, so confirm the match before ordering:

- check the year, make, model, and engine
- compare the connector shape and mounting points with the coil you remove
- use the original part number as a reference

## The short version

Misfires, a rough idle, hesitation, and a check engine light are the classic signs of a failing ignition coil. Read the codes, swap the suspect coil to another cylinder, and see whether the misfire follows it. If it does, replace the coil, and consider the set and the plugs on a higher-mileage engine.
