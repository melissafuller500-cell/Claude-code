## Where the money gets stuck

- **Progress statements waiting weeks for approval.** Until it is signed, the statement cannot be invoiced, and the payment clock does not start.
- **Retention money, forgotten and never claimed.** It is released months after the site closes, when the team is already elsewhere.
- **Extra work done without a written order or amendment.** When payment falls due, the client disputes it, and you have nothing to prove it.

## The clauses that matter

- **A signed monthly statement** from the project owner or their representative, with an approval deadline written in the contract.
- **The release date of each retention**, entered in the tracker, with a reminder on that date.
- **No extra work without a written, priced order.** One line in the contract is enough: any extra work requires a signed written order before it starts.
- **Proof of delivery**: a signed acceptance report, with a deadline for written reservations.

The payment-terms template offers this wording, to adapt and have reviewed.

## Worked example

*Fictional example.* A finishing-works company invoices 12,000,000 FCFA of progress statements per month. Its statements wait 20 days on average before approval, then are paid at 45 days: the money arrives 65 days after the work. If a 5-day approval deadline is written in the contract and respected, it gains 15 days, i.e. 12,000,000 ÷ 30 × 15 = **6,000,000 FCFA** of cash freed.

On a 40,000,000 FCFA contract with 5% retention, **2,000,000 FCFA** wait for final acceptance. A date in the tracker stops them from being forgotten.
