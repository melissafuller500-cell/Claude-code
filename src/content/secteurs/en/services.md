## Where the money gets stuck

- **Recurring services invoiced without proof they were performed.** The client pays what they can check; the rest waits.
- **A quality complaint that appears when the invoice arrives.** Never during the month, always when it is time to pay.
- **An invoice sent to the wrong department**, sitting in a drawer while you think it is being paid.

## The clauses that matter

- **A job sheet or attendance sheet signed every month** by the client’s site manager.
- **A written complaint window in the contract**: after it, the service is deemed accepted.
- **The approver’s name and the invoice route**, recorded at signing: who receives it, who approves it, who pays it.
- **Payment terms in days** from receipt of the invoice, repeated on every invoice.

The reminder sent five days before the due date checks that the invoice has been registered: it is the first step of the reminder schedule.

## Worked example

*Fictional example.* A security company invoices 6,000,000 FCFA a month to four clients. Its invoices are paid at 75 days on average, against 30-day terms. The share due to late payment is 6,000,000 ÷ 30 × 45 = **9,000,000 FCFA** tied up. Financed by a 14% overdraft, it costs about **1,260,000 FCFA a year**.
