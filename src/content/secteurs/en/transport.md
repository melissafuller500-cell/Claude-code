## Where the money gets stuck

- **The signed waybill, lost between the driver and the office.** Without it, delivery is disputed and the invoice waits.
- **Disputed extra charges**: waiting time, handling, tolls, when they were not agreed in writing.
- **Monthly batch invoicing**, which starts the payment clock three or four weeks after the trip.

## The clauses that matter

- **The signed waybill, photographed and sent to the office the same day**, over WhatsApp.
- **A schedule of extra charges attached to the contract**: hourly waiting rate beyond a free period, handling, road costs.
- **Invoicing at delivery or every two weeks**, rather than one monthly invoice.
- **Payment terms in days from the invoice date**, and a reminder on a fixed date.

## Worked example

*Fictional example.* A haulier invoices 9,000,000 FCFA a month, in a single invoice on the 30th. A trip made on the 2nd is only invoiced 28 days later, then paid at 30 days: the money arrives 58 days after delivery. By invoicing every two weeks, it gains 7 to 8 days on average, roughly 9,000,000 ÷ 30 × 7 = **2,100,000 FCFA** of cash freed.
