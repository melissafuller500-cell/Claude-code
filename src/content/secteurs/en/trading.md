## Where the money gets stuck

- **Credit granted with no limit.** Yesterday’s good client becomes today’s largest debtor, without anyone deciding it.
- **Goods delivered without a signed note.** When payment falls due, quantities are disputed.
- **A client who reorders while already owing.** Each new delivery increases the risk instead of reducing it.

## The clauses that matter

- **A credit limit per client**, written down and checked every week in the unpaid-invoices tracker.
- **Retention of title in the contract** and **a signed delivery note for every delivery**.
- **Deliveries paused above the limit, no exceptions**, announced in your terms of sale so the rule surprises no one.
- **Payment terms in days** and the late-payment penalty, written on the purchase order and on the invoice.

## Worked example

*Fictional example.* A building-materials trader invoices 15,000,000 FCFA a month. One large client has built up 7,500,000 FCFA of unpaid invoices, the equivalent of 15 days of the whole company’s revenue. With a limit set at 3,000,000 FCFA, deliveries would have been paused much earlier, and the exposure limited to **3,000,000 FCFA** instead of **7,500,000 FCFA**.
